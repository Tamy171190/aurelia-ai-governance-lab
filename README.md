# Aurelia AI Governance Lab

A practical AI Governance and Assurance portfolio built around a fictional international private-markets and asset-management group, Aurelia Capital Group.

## Purpose

This lab demonstrates how AI Governance principles can be translated into practical processes, controls, evidence, risk assessments and assurance activities across financial services.

The portfolio uses a multi-jurisdiction design covering the United Kingdom, European Union, Singapore and United Arab Emirates, with ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF used as cross-framework governance baselines. Regulatory requirements are clearly distinguished from illustrative methodology and design choices used in this fictional environment.

## Aurelia Capital Group

A fictional mid-sized international private-markets and asset-management group with approximately $3-5bn AUM and institutional clients, operating across:

- United Kingdom
- European Union
- Singapore
- United Arab Emirates

Business areas include private equity, asset management, fund administration, investment research, investor relations, finance, risk & compliance, HR and technology.

The organisation is deliberately designed to be sophisticated enough to require formal AI governance, but not so large or systemically important that every AI use case is automatically treated as high risk.

## Multi-Jurisdictional AIMS Design Principle

The AIMS is designed to identify the requirements applicable to each AI system and then establish a harmonised control baseline wherever possible.

**Requirement -> Applicability -> Highest Applicable Control Requirement -> Aurelia Control -> Evidence -> Testing -> Monitoring**

The project uses a **control ceiling** principle: where multiple applicable frameworks address the same risk, Aurelia designs to the strongest applicable control requirement where practical. Jurisdiction-specific obligations that cannot be harmonised are retained as explicit enhancements or exceptions.

This is not a claim that the strictest regulation automatically applies everywhere. Legal applicability must be assessed for each AI system, activity, entity, data flow and jurisdiction.

## AI Governance Lifecycle

**Discover -> Inventory -> Classify -> Assess -> Map -> Govern -> Control -> Implement -> Monitor -> Assure -> Report**

## Portfolio Roadmap

1. **AI Systems Inventory & Classification Engine** - identify AI systems, capture governance attributes, determine jurisdictional applicability indicators and apply an illustrative risk-classification methodology.
2. **AI Risk Assessment Engine** - assess inherent and residual AI risks and define treatment requirements.
3. **AI Governance Framework** - establish governance principles, roles, decision rights and oversight.
4. **AI Control Framework** - map risks and requirements to harmonised controls, jurisdictional enhancements and evidence.
5. **ISO/IEC 42001 Alignment** - demonstrate how an AI management system can be structured against the standard.
6. **EU AI Act Assessment** - create a fictional regulatory-readiness and applicability assessment workflow.
7. **AI Incident Management** - simulate detection, triage, escalation, remediation and reporting.
8. **Third-Party AI Risk** - assess external foundation models and AI vendors across jurisdictions.
9. **AI Conformity Assessment** - develop evidence-based assessment for selected use cases where conformity or enhanced assurance is relevant.
10. **AI Governance Audit** - independently test governance design and operating effectiveness.
11. **Board AI Governance Dashboard** - translate AI risk information into senior-management reporting.
12. **AI Governance Stress Tests** - deliberately challenge the framework using realistic financial-services scenarios.
13. **Multi-Jurisdictional AIMS & Regulatory Mapping** - maintain the master cross-framework matrix, control ceiling logic, applicability assessment and jurisdiction-specific enhancements.
14. **AI Governance Evidence Pack & Audit Readiness** - connect requirements, controls and evidence so the AIMS can be independently assessed.

## Design Principles

- Financial-services context
- Evidence-based governance
- Risk-based classification
- Multi-jurisdiction applicability assessment
- Highest applicable control requirement
- Human oversight
- Accountability and traceability
- Regulatory awareness without assuming universal applicability
- Harmonised global controls with jurisdiction-specific enhancements
- Practical implementation
- Independent assurance

## Important Note

This is a fictional learning and demonstration environment. It does not represent Aurelia Capital Group or any real organisation, and it is not legal, regulatory or compliance advice. Framework and regulatory mappings are illustrative unless explicitly identified as source-based requirements and should be validated against current official materials before real-world use.
