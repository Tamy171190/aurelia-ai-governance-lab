# AI System Classification Engine

## Objective

Demonstrate how a financial-services organisation can move from AI inventory intake to a consistent, evidence-oriented governance classification.

The engine is deliberately designed as a governance decision-support methodology, not as a legal classification tool.

## Aurelia scenarios

The portfolio includes fictional financial-services use cases covering HR, KYC, credit risk, AML, NAV operations, private-equity valuation, investor communications, contracts, HR assistance and board reporting.

## Classification inputs

The engine considers:

- Intended purpose and business function
- Deployment geography
- Personal and sensitive data
- Consequential decision use
- Employment use
- Credit or financial decision use
- Customer-facing use
- Third-party AI dependency
- Autonomy level
- Human override capability
- Material impact

## Illustrative output tiers

### High Governance Attention

Triggered by consequential employment or credit / financial decision use, high material impact, or insufficient human override in a consequential decision.

Expected response includes enhanced risk assessment, documented human oversight, testing, monitoring, evidence and jurisdiction-specific applicability review.

### Enhanced Governance

Used where the combined governance indicators show material, data-intensive, customer-facing, third-party or semi-autonomous use that requires controls beyond the core AIMS baseline.

### Standard Governance

Core AIMS controls apply, with applicable jurisdictional requirements assessed and documented.

## Control ceiling principle

Where multiple applicable frameworks address the same risk, Aurelia uses the strongest applicable control requirement as the harmonised baseline where practical. Requirements that cannot be harmonised are retained as jurisdiction-specific enhancements or exceptions.

This does not mean that the strictest regulation automatically applies everywhere. Applicability remains a separate assessment based on the system, entity, activity, data flow and jurisdiction.

## Framework alignment

The classification output feeds the wider Aurelia governance lifecycle:

**Inventory -> Classify -> Risk Assess -> Regulatory Map -> Control -> Evidence -> Test -> Monitor**

The design is aligned conceptually to ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF, while jurisdictional requirements are assessed separately for the EU, UK, Singapore and UAE.

## Learning objective

The project demonstrates a practical capability to answer a real governance question:

> Given an AI system, what governance intensity should apply, why, what requirements should be assessed, and what controls and evidence should follow?

## Disclaimer

Aurelia Capital Group is fictional. The classification methodology is illustrative and does not constitute legal, regulatory or compliance advice. Current official requirements should be validated before real-world use.
