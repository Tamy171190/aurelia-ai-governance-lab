# AI Inventory and Classification Methodology

## Purpose

This project demonstrates a practical approach to maintaining an enterprise AI inventory and applying a repeatable, risk-based governance classification.

## Inventory Principle

An AI system is registered before governance classification is performed. Inventory and classification are treated as separate activities so that the organisation can establish a complete population before determining the level of oversight required.

## Risk Factors

The illustrative engine considers:

1. Impact on individuals
2. Investment or financial decision impact
3. Financial impact
4. Regulatory significance
5. Sensitive data
6. Autonomy
7. Explainability challenge
8. Third-party dependency
9. Cross-border data processing

## Illustrative Governance Tiers

- Low: baseline inventory, ownership, documentation and periodic review
- Moderate: formal risk assessment, human oversight, monitoring and change management
- High: enhanced assessment, documented human oversight, enhanced documentation, approval, monitoring, incident management, third-party assessment where relevant, and independent assurance consideration

The numerical scoring and thresholds in this project are **fictional design choices for demonstration purposes**. They are not presented as requirements of ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, Singapore guidance, UAE requirements or any other regulation.

## Risk Score

The engine generates an illustrative inherent risk score. A high score is a governance prioritisation signal, not a legal determination.

## Regulatory Flags

Regulatory flags are deliberately separated from the numerical score. Deployment in a jurisdiction, use of personal data, financial impact, generative AI and third-party dependency may trigger further legal or governance assessment.

## Human Oversight

Human oversight is recorded as an inventory attribute. The presence of a human reviewer does not automatically make a system low risk. The quality, independence, authority and effectiveness of the oversight must be assessed separately.

## Limitations

This is a fictional portfolio project. It does not provide legal advice or determine actual regulatory classification. A production implementation would require jurisdiction-specific legal analysis, validated risk criteria, stakeholder approval, data-quality controls, model validation and governance ownership.
